# Changelog

All notable changes to this mod are listed here, newest first.
Each release gets a dated entry with Added / Changed / Fixed / Removed sections.

## Unreleased

### Added

- **Hound dens** are a Level 1 place now (2 to 4 per map, "hound den" on the map once seen), in four layouts: a den with two or three Hounds at home, an abandoned den with dry bones and maybe a fang, a survivor's last stand (their body and gear, spent shells, two Hounds still around) and a feeding ground (bone heaps, a half-eaten body, a big pack and shed fangs). Dot's "Hound den" job sends you to one you haven't seen within 30 squares and adds a pack of three.
- The Exchange has **three guards** now: one by the gate and two in the back corners. Each wears body armour and an army helmet, fights with a steel spear (it reaches over the fence) or a machete, and carries a loaded Glock, with the strength and skill to use them.
- **Survivor Minds traces** (with Survivor Minds on too; Backrooms Expanded still works without it): the dead survivor at an overrun camp carries their **journal**, one of four, with the last days before the camp fell (read it like any found journal). Survivor Minds' chalk marks appear on their own: **CAMP** and **WATER** arrows outside squatter camps and Level 2 tunnel crew camps, **DANGER** where a dead zone meets the lit garage, and **LOOTED** by an overrun camp's picked-over crates. About 1 in 5 maintenance rooms, attendant booths and security offices on Level 1 has been picked over: emptied, with a LOOTED mark at the door.
- Backrooms entities have weaknesses. **Smilers burn in bright light:** switch on a flashlight, or catch one by a lamp or fire, and it sizzles and loses about a quarter of its health every turn, dying in a few turns (Level 0's ceiling lights are too dim; needs the Xbro10 fork build). **Hounds** have low morale: stand your ground and hurt them, kill one, or bring light or fire, and the pack breaks. **Deathmoths** are not scared of fire, so they fly into flares and fires, and burning weapons do triple damage to their wings. **Wretches** are dry husks: burning weapons do double damage. **Skin-stealers** back off from bright light.
- The **Flamero-Mado spear** in the Backrooms: with Additions on too (the spear's own mod, on by default in new worlds), it turns up in Level 2 crew stores, as the Exchange oddities trader's rare find, and in the Redeemer's remains. Backrooms Expanded works with or without it.
- Four finds from the Backrooms Wiki, each with its own sprite: **moth jelly** (sweet food that mends a few hit points; Deathmoths often leave a glob or two), **firesalt** (warm red crystals that burn as lamp fuel, so they also fill oil lamps and the Flamero-Mado spear; Level 2 junk heaps and crew stores), **royal rations** (a light, never-spoiling bar worth most of a day's food; rare in Level 1 food finds and the Manila Room) and **cloudy water** (liquid pain: it comes in an almond water bottle, but drinking it hurts badly; Level 2 flooded rooms and hound dens).
- **Firesalt tools** (craftable): a **firesalt torch** that burns twice as long as a fuel torch and a little brighter, and **firesalt twists**, a quick fire-starter good for about five fires (followers who light fires can use them too). Sprites for both.
- The new finds are part of trade: the Exchange's supplies stall sells moth jelly and the odd royal ration (and pays well for rations), the gear stall sells and buys firesalt, and every Level 2 tunnel crew member carries a pouch of firesalt to trade. No trader buys cloudy water ("That isn't almond water.").
- NPCs never drink cloudy water on their own; with Survivor Minds, those you hand it to write about it.
- **Entity loot**, each with a sprite: hounds sometimes leave a fang or two; a Smiler that fades sometimes leaves its teeth; Facelings drop the ordinary clothes and pocket things they were carrying (a wallet, a snack, now and then almond water); a Skin-stealer leaves the human skin it wore (it tans into leather); every Partygoer leaves its red balloon, often a party hat, sometimes a slice of cake; the Redeemer always leaves a cracked insulator from its pole. Wretches sometimes carry a lost note. The Exchange's oddities stall buys fangs, teeth, insulators and balloons, and pays extra for the trophies.

### Changed

- Fewer brown rats on Level 1: they were the most common spawn, in packs of up to five, and slowed the level down. Now a smaller share, in packs of one to three (one or two in dead zones).
- Breakroom runners are the camp's best fighters: high melee, cutting, dodge and pistol skills, a machete in a scabbard, a loaded Glock 19 with spare magazines, a leather jacket, a first aid kit, bandages and painkillers. A runner badly hurt on a run (torso or head under half, or in a lot of pain) cuts it short and heads home; otherwise a run lasts until 1-2 days after leaving. Runners check how hurt they are every 10 minutes (it was every 30), so they turn back soon after a bad fight. The diner server and the Exchange traders keep the runners' old everyday clothes.
- The All-Nite serves chili and soup in a bowl and coffee in a styrofoam cup. Chili used to spawn as a loose puddle (it has no container of its own), soup came in a can and coffee in a plastic bottle.
- The pistol in a security office's gun safe has its part-loaded magazine in the gun, instead of the gun and magazine lying side by side.
- Breakroom runners start with a loaded magazine in their Glock and two spares, instead of an empty gun and three loose magazines.
- Trading overhaul at the Exchange and the All-Nite diner. Traders and the cook only sell their stall stock, never the gun, magazines, clothes or flashlight they carry. Each has a clear trade with fewer kinds of goods in deeper piles: supplies (almond water, beans, granola, crackers, bandages, aspirin, disinfectant, purifying tablets), gear (battery cells, flashlights, glowsticks, lighters, tape, rope, a few tools), oddities (maps, guides, generator notes, markers, plus one rare find each restock), and the cook (about five dishes a day, three or four portions each, plus coffee and almond water). The crates behind the Exchange stalls no longer start full of random camp stores.
- Each trader says what they sell ("What do you sell?", or "What's cooking today?" for the cook), and a sign at each stall and a menu board in the diner list it. Traders buy only their own kind of goods ("only deals in food, water and medicine"), pay a quarter or more extra for it, and sell it a little cheaper.

### Fixed

- Deathmoths really burn in fire now: they're flying insects without the insect fire fear, and their powdery wings are flammable (a ground fire barely hurts most flying monsters).
- Skin-stealers (and Hounds) back off when a flashlight, lamp or fire lights them up (needs the Xbro10 fork build). The game's own bright-light fear only counted near-daylight, so a flashlight in a dark room never scared them.
- Dot's "Hound den" job no longer fails with "Unable to find and assign mission target".
- "Who they were": the overrun camp it marks always has its body with the roster (it used to be able to turn a square you had already seen into the camp, which left the roster on bare floor).
- The 3-job and 6-job rewards (the stores key, Marcus's map) wait until you talk to Dot ("You wanted to see me?") instead of popping up out in the lanes when a job finishes there.
- Breakroom door guards carry a steel spear (Exchange guards usually do too): it reaches over the chain-link fence, so they now fight zombies at the fence instead of ignoring them.
- The runner board says "back for a rest" when a runner on a run has come home to sleep, instead of still saying OUT.
- No more "cannot contain it" error when a reward or the weekly ration gives two or three almond waters: each comes in its own bottle.
- Butchering Backrooms entities no longer gives human flesh. Hounds give stringy mutant meat, fat, bone, sinew and a hide; Deathmoths a little mutant meat; the Redeemer a great deal of it. Only Wretches and Facelings, which were or look like people, still butcher as people.
- Breakroom runners really leave now. They looked for a squatter camp, lobby or generator room only 12 squares away, and those are rare, so most days they found nothing and the board said "Done for today". They now look 30 squares out, and if there is still nothing they go scavenging in the lanes ("OUT, scavenging the lanes").
- Breakroom Bunker: the two archers now stand in half-sandbag nests either side of the airlock instead of in the open at the ends of the sandbag wall.
- Breakroom archers carry a combat knife in a sheath and wear a bow sling. When something gets close they draw the knife and sling the bow, instead of dropping the bow and fighting with an arrow.
- Breakroom archers are always strong enough to draw their bow. Every bow they carry needs Strength 9, and some archers rolled less, so they tried to wield it over and over ("needs at least strength 9 to use this"). They get +5 Strength now, and a little stabbing and melee skill for their knife.
- Exchange stalls no longer mix their stock. All three traders shared one faction, so restocks and the trade window used the same loot zones: the supplies trader listed the gear stall's tools, and his food landed elsewhere. Each trader now has their own stall faction and loot zone (the stall crates and counter), and the stall crates and counters no longer get random camp loot. The diner cook trades from the counter, not the fridge and cupboards. The free field guide is no longer sold.

## v0.1.0 - 2026-11-17

### Added
- The Breakroom has **archers** and a **supply depot**. Two archers with bows (recurve, compound or long) and quivers of arrows watch the front: from the sandbag nests in the Checkpoint's yard, or behind the Bunker's sandbag wall. The depot (racks and crates along the Checkpoint's yard, racks in the Bunker's workshop) holds food, almond water, medicine, tools, lights and batteries, owned by the Breakroom, and every runner who gets home brings a fresh batch.
- The Backrooms' entities (Smilers, Hounds, Deathmoths, Wretches, Skin-stealers, Partygoers and the Redeemer) are their own faction now: they never fight each other, always attack zombies and other outsiders that wander in, and leave Facelings alone.
- Sprites (UltiCa and UndeadPeople) for the newest things: the Manila Room's cream wallpaper walls (connecting like the other Level 0 walls), deep carpet, dark soggy carpet and pooled fluid, and map signs for the Exchange (a gold coin under a market awning) and the All-Nite diner (a burger on a plate).
- **The All-Nite**, a diner on Level 1 (one or two per map; a red "D" on the map once seen): a cook who sells hot meals from the grill (burgers, pancakes, chili, coffee and more, fresh every day), a server who hears all the gossip, and regulars at the counter and in the booths. Its fridge and cupboards are stocked and owned by the diner, and there is a water tap in the kitchen, so with NPC autonomy on it is where people come to eat and talk.
- **The Exchange**, a market on Level 1 (one or two per map, found by exploring; "$" on the map once seen): three traders behind counters (supplies: food, almond water and medicine; gear: tools, hardware, lights and batteries; oddities: maps, notes and field guides), a locked storeroom, a lit aisle with tables, all inside a chain-link fence with a gate and a guard. Stock refreshes every few days. Almond water is the closest thing to money: it sells high and buys a lot. The traders also share rumours.
- More kinds of space on **Level 0** (from the Backrooms Wiki): **archway rooms** (rings of arches and pillars over deep, squelching carpet that slows you down, open on every side, with dead-end alcoves and one light in the middle), **blackout zones** (unlit rooms of dark, soggy carpet with ankle-deep pools of foul fluid; Smilers like them) and **pillar halls** (a whole square of open carpet with thick pillars every 4 tiles, about 1 square in 17).
- The **Manila Room** (Level 0, from the Backrooms Wiki): a rare, small, dry room with manila-coloured walls, a sofa, a bedroll and a cupboard. It always has ready-to-eat food in the cupboard and almond water on the table, plus a few random finds (medical supplies, a light, tools, batteries). About one in every 60 Level 0 squares has one.
- The Breakroom has a **larder** its people own: ready-to-eat food on the kitchen counters and almond water and bottled water in the fridge, enough for a few days. With NPC autonomy on, Dot, Marcus, the runners and the guard eat and drink from it; taking from it yourself counts as stealing. Each time a runner gets home, a fresh batch goes in (old Breakrooms too). The Breakroom's crates and lockers are also stocked far more often.
- The **Breakroom** is bigger and comes in one of two layouts, picked when it is built: the **Checkpoint** (a guard room and an infirmary, then a lit, chain-link yard with sandbag nests and a gate off to the side, so visitors cross the light) or the **Bunker** (a workshop and extra bunks, then an airlock between two doors with barred side windows, a sandbag wall and barbed wire with one gap). Both have a **door guard** to talk to. Existing worlds keep the Breakroom they already have.
- A **runner board** by the Breakroom door. Every midnight Dot chalks up when Jonah and Rosa leave that day (6 AM to 5 PM, different every day); read it to see who is in, who is out and where, who is overdue, and when the next one goes.
- Survival Difficulty Classes for every level, using the Backrooms Wiki's 0-to-5 rating (our own wording). **Level 0** "the Lobby" and **Level 1** "the Parking Deck" are Class 1 (safe, secure); **Level 2** "the Pipe Tunnels" is Class 3 (unsafe, unstable, moderate entity count). The first time you reach each level a card pops up with its class and what to expect; later visits log a one-line reminder. Every scenario's description now ends with its level's class and what to expect.
- The **wanderer's field guide**, a passed-around notebook that every Backrooms scenario starts with. Read it ("Look up this level") to see the card for the level you are on.
- **Pipe Rat**, a hard new start (-2 points): alone on Level 2 after the stairwell collapses behind you, with a flashlight, a wrench, one granola bar and one bottle of almond water.
- Exits are for players to find. No scenario description, class card or field guide page says how to get from one level to another; only people and notes in the game drop hints.
- Level 2 is unstable: every 3 to 6 hours there, a pipe bursts a few tiles from you, filling the spot with scalding steam for about 10 minutes. The noise carries.
- Level 0 has a few of the Backrooms' own entities now: Facelings, the odd Hound pair, Smilers, Deathmoths, and after day 30 a rare Wretch, about 1 spawn in 70 (everything else is still the dead and animals from outside).
- Breakroom runners. Jonah Pike and Rosa Ibarra live in the Breakroom, and Dot sends one at a time on a run most days, at the time on the runner board: to a squatter camp, an elevator lobby or a generator room 3 to 12 squares away. They really walk there and back across Level 1 (only while you are on Level 1), so you can meet them in the lanes and ask what they are doing or what it is like out there. Each run that comes home puts a little extra food in the Breakroom supply locker for your next weekly draw. About 1 run in 10 goes wrong: the runner doesn't come back, and Dot offers a new job, **Find the runner**, with the place marked on your map. Talk to them to get them walking home (sometimes Hounds heard you), then tell Dot. Ask Dot "Where are the runners?" to see who is out.
- Level 1 lights come and go. Lit lane lights near you flicker out for a couple of seconds now and then ("Overhead, a light buzzes and stutters."), and every so often one dies for good, so the lanes you live in slowly get darker and Smilers get more room. The generator can still bring dead lights back for a week. Rarely, while you stand in an elevator lobby, every lobby light goes out at once for half an hour to an hour, and sometimes a Smiler comes to the dark. Lobby and generator lights never flicker or fail on their own.
- Three new Backrooms entities with their own sprites (UltiCa and UndeadPeople). **Facelings**: people in plain clothes with blank, featureless faces; they wander, leave everything alone and only fight back when hurt (Level 1 and Level 2, uncommon). **Skin-stealers**: tall grey things wearing a human skin like a coat, calling out in a human voice ("Hello? Is someone there?", "I found water!") to draw you in, then hitting hard (Level 1 only, rare, after day 20). **Partygoers**: tall, thin mustard-yellow figures with ridged skin, slit eyes, a wide red grin, mouths for hands and a red balloon, very fast and very strong, inviting you to the party (Level 1 and Level 2, very rare, after day 40).
- The Redeemer (boss, not placed in any level yet): a car-sized, three-headed wolf-like beast with a wooden power pole growing out of its back, wires running from it into the fur and current crackling between the insulators. It has its own large 128x96 sprite (it stands on its tile and towers over the tiles around it) for UltiCa and UndeadPeople, placeholder stats with a shock storm and an electric bite, and can be spawned from the debug menu (`mon_br_redeemer`). Teleporting comes when it gets a level.
- Breakroom standing. Every finished Breakroom job counts. **3 jobs:** Dot gives you the Breakroom supply key, which opens a new supply locker in the Breakroom stores for a weekly ration (almond water, food, first aid, sometimes a battery or light), and you get a "Breakroom regular" morale boost when you hand in jobs. **6 jobs:** Marcus gives you his hand-drawn map (copy it onto yours to reveal nearby lobbies, camps and a generator room), Dot gives you three bottles of almond water from her own tin, and the camp trusts you more. Ask Dot "Where do I stand with you lot?" to see where you are.
- Security office gun safe: the guard office now has a lockable gun safe (pick or pry it). About 2 in 5 hold a duty sidearm (Glock 19, M1911 or a .38 revolver) with a magazine and a little ammo; some hold only loose rounds.
- Oil-stained notes: a rarer note with a hand-drawn sketch. Activate it on Level 1 and it marks a generator room on your map, 3 to 8 squares away (on squares you haven't seen yet), with its own amber map sign. Half the time someone left a can of gas there too. Each note leads somewhere once. The old crumpled note about the generator now says the writer never found it again.
- Level 0 map sprites (UltiCa and UndeadPeople): the yellow rooms seen from above (three layouts of walls, doorways and lights), the border wall, the stairs (a staircase with an up or down arrow) and a signboard for the rippling-wall room.
- Level 0 furniture and item sprites (UltiCa and UndeadPeople): the water cooler (white cabinet, blue jug), the waycircle (a boulder with a painted glyph, candles and a bone), the Level 0 rippling wall (wallpaper whose stripes bend like cloth), the Level 1 sealed concrete patch, and almond water and its bottle (milky water, paper label).
- Level 0 wall and floor sprites (UltiCa and UndeadPeople): yellow wallpaper walls and grey walls that join properly at corners and junctions, showing their papered face only on the side you look at, damp yellow carpet with three stain variants, carpet and tile under buzzing ceiling-light panels, and faded tile. Level 0 used to borrow the base game's yellow wall and carpet.
- Entity sprites for the Smiler (a shadow with a glowing grin), Hound (a pale person on all fours), Deathmoth (a giant moth with eyespots) and Wretch (a skinless, raw-red figure with glowing yellow eyes, showing ribs and curved bone claws; its description now matches). Original pixel art in the style of UndeadPeople, used with UltiCa or UndeadPeople; other tilesets keep the old look-alike sprites.
- Level 2 map art, notes and graffiti. In UltiCa the tunnels (two variants), service ladders, boiler rooms, valve junctions, flooded dips and Tunnel Crew camps each have their own overmap sprite. Sweat-stained notes turn up at the landing, in junk heaps and in boiler lockers, and paint on the walls points the way (not always truthfully).
- Level 2 spawns and loot. Rats in the pipes, Hound packs in the corridors, Smilers in the long dark stretches, Deathmoths at the caged bulbs (sometimes already waiting at one), a few sluggish zombies, and later cockroaches and Wretches. Heaps of junk along the corridors hold pipe, fittings, scrap, tools and the odd battery; food is rarer than on Level 1.
- The Tunnel Crew: Level 2's only people. 1 to 3 camps per map in old pump rooms (bunks, a cold boiler they cook on, a drip pool for water, stores) with 2 to 4 crew who talk, trade and can be recruited. They explain the tunnels and the BLEED wheels and pass on rumours of a way further down. They offer no jobs; only the Breakroom does.
- Level 2 structure variants. Boiler rooms can be **cold** (dead boilers, someone camped there; cooler, with their stores and a note) or a **pump room** (two boilers, banks of pumps and its own BLEED wheel). Valve junctions can be a **burst main** (six joints hissing and a body short of the wheel) or a **control closet** (the wheel in a side room). Flooded dips can hold a **drowned traveller** with their gear, or be **draining** (just a pool at the crossing).
- Level 2 places. **Boiler rooms** (3 to 6 per map): a room of roaring boilers, the hottest spot on the level, with lockers of tools, batteries and fuel. **Valve junctions** (4 to 8 per map): turn the BLEED wheel (slow and loud) to shut every leaking steam joint within 20 tiles for 2 days. **Flooded dips** (2 to 4 per map): the tunnels stand in warm, knee-deep water, with sunken things to fish out.
- Level 2 heat and water. The tunnels run hot all year (about 30 C, rarely below 20 C, spikes past 40 C) with close, wet air, so you sweat, get thirsty and can get heat stroke. Leaking steam joints make the air around them 10 to 20 C hotter still. Condensation drips into pools in the tunnels (never run dry, boil before drinking).
- Level 2, Pipe Dreams (first step): a new dimension of hot, narrow maintenance tunnels, 3 tiles wide between pipe-choked walls, laid out as a maze of loops and dead ends that runs on across map squares, lit only by the odd caged bulb. **Way in:** about 1 Level 1 stairwell in 4 is still open; take the stairs down and they collapse behind you. **Way back:** rare service ladders (2 to 4 per map) climb to a floor hatch on Level 1. Followers within 20 tiles come along both ways. Heat, steam, places, people and spawns come in the next steps.
- Siphonable cars on Level 1: about 1 parked car in 4 still has a little fuel left (a few litres), enough to siphon for a generator or two. The rest are bone dry.
- Breakroom jobs. Ask Dot "Need a hand with anything?" or Marcus "Got any work for me?". **Dot**: *Fuel run* (bring 5 litres of gasoline, repeatable every couple of days), *Thin the Hounds* (kill 3 Hounds anywhere, repeatable), *The Hound den* (a den of four Hounds marked on your map; clear it), *Who they were* (bring back the roster from an overrun camp). **Marcus**: *Supply drop* (a padlocked cage with a sealed Breakroom crate; bring it back unopened), *Ride the lifts* (three elevator rides that end in a lobby; he reveals two more lobbies, repeatable). Rewards are supplies, almond water and Breakroom standing.
- Elevator lobbies on Level 1 (3 to 6 per map): a walled, brightly lit lobby with benches, a lost-and-found bin that refills about once a week, and sometimes a traveller waiting for the lift. Nothing spawns inside and Smilers won't enter the light, so they make good places to camp. Each elevator has a panel: **any floor** (a random lobby 5 to 30 squares away, marked on your map), **back** (the lobby you came from) and **home** (a lobby you've marked). Followers within 6 tiles ride with you, and every arrival chimes loud enough for nearby things to hear. Every ride is a small gamble, with a pop-up saying what happened: most are normal, but you can get the **wrong floor** (a dead zone with no way back), get **stuck** (an hour lost, thirsty, tired and shaken), have **company** (a Smiler in the car or a Hound pack drawn by the chime), find a **lucky floor** (a sealed storeroom nobody has looted), open on a **dark lobby** (power dead, blood, something still inside) or, about 1 ride in 50, find the **way out** to Level 0. Lobbies have their own map sprite in UltiCa, and a couple of new notes mention them.
- Level 1 useful places. **Security office**: a locked room with dead CCTV, the guard's lockers (flashlight, baton, batteries) and their logbook. **Generator rooms**: pour in two litres of gasoline and every dead ceiling light within 12 tiles comes back on for a week; sometimes a can of gas was left behind. **Storage cages**: padlocked chain-link lockups with the best tool loot on the level.
- Level 1 story structures. **Dead zones**: 2x2 patches of garage where every light has died; more Smilers and Hounds, more abandoned cars, and sometimes an old camp's stash worth the risk. **Overrun camps**: a squatter camp that didn't make it, with blood, a body, their gear and a last note, and sometimes a Wretch still there. **The exit ramp that isn't**: EXIT signs and a ramp up into a fresh brick wall with days tallied on it. **Graffiti**: spray-painted arrows and warnings from earlier travellers, some helpful, some not. Dead zones and overrun camps have their own map sprites in UltiCa.
- Notes on Level 1. Most arrival alcoves have a torn note left by someone who came before: one or two hints, never the whole picture, and not all of them true. Crumpled notes with bits of other people's stories turn up in lockers, junk and camp stores.
- Level 1 entities, all weaker than a zombie, with no grabs and packs of 3 at most so fights stay short. **Smiler**: a grin you only see up close in the dark; it flees bright light and keeps to the edge of yours. **Hound**: packs of 2-3 that hunt by sound and scatter when one dies. **Deathmoth**: weak swarms that gather at working lights (sometimes waiting at one). **Wretch** (day 30+): a survivor who stayed too long; slow, clumsy, and drops their old gear. Level 1 now spawns fewer rats and zombies to make room.
- Level 1 map art for UltiCa: the parking deck (three variants, some with parked cars or dead lights), arrival alcoves (a cyan rippling wall), the Breakroom (walled, with a green door) and squatter camps (a sandbag ring around a brazier) now each have their own overmap sprite. Other tilesets keep the old look.
- Three scenarios that start on Level 1. **Wrong Exit**: you start in an arrival alcove with your out-of-gas car in the lane outside, plus a flashlight and some road-trip food. **Breakroom Hire** (1 point): you start inside the Breakroom next to the Foreman, the Breakroom likes and trusts you, and the nearest squatter camp is marked on your map. **One of the Lost** (-1 point): you start inside a squatter camp with its survivors, the Lost like you, and the Breakroom is cooler towards you. Upstream's two Backrooms scenarios are still available.
- Almond water on Level 1: rare in attendant booths, camp stores and about 1 in 10 drinks machines, and Dot gives newcomers their first bottle. Drinking it now also calms you (a gentle mood boost for about 4 hours). This extends the Backrooms mod's own almond water rather than replacing it.
- Water on Level 1: drip pools under leaking pipes and inside every squatter camp (never run dry; boil before drinking), and two rigged taps of clean water in the Breakroom. NPCs within 6 tiles drink from them on their own.
- Vending alcoves: one or two stocked vending machines (snacks and drinks) under a working light. Pay with a cash card or break in.
- The Breakroom: a walled survivor hub on Level 1 with Dot Kowalczyk ("the Foreman"), who explains Level 1, and Marcus "Mile Marker" Reyes, who keeps the maps, can mark the nearest camp for you, and has rumours of what lies beyond. It forms once per world, 4 to 10 squares from your first arrival, and is marked on your map.
- Squatter camps: 4 to 8 sandbagged camps per map with 2 to 4 random lost survivors who can be talked to, traded with and recruited, each with their own story of falling through.
- Two factions: The Breakroom and The Lost. Each owns its camp's stores, so taking from them counts as stealing.
- Level 1, the Parking Deck: a new dimension of endless concrete parking garage (pillars, faded bay lines, ceiling lights that may be dead).
- Level 1 variety: puddles, leaking pipe runs, junk piles, guaranteed working lights, maintenance and electrical rooms, an attendant booth (best odds of food and drink), sealed stairwells with misleading signs, and the odd wrecked car.
- Level 1 loot (tools, car parts, hardware, batteries; food and water are rare) and monsters (mostly rats, some zombies; cockroaches after day 45).
- Rippling walls: a rare room on the ground floor of Level 0 (3 to 6 per map) whose back wall you can step through into Level 1. Followers within 5 tiles come along.
- Level 1 arrival alcoves. The patch of wall you fell through is sealed concrete: there is no going back the way you came. The only way back to Level 0 is a rare elevator ride.
- Mod skeleton: modinfo.json, README and this changelog.

### Changed
- Level 0 runs faster: its ceiling lights shine at brightness 40 instead of 120 (overriding the upstream Backrooms terrain). Light barely fades in open air, so each light reached about 92 tiles and the thousands of them took most of each turn's map update. The floor still reads as brightly lit everywhere, and lighting takes about 40% less time.
- Breakroom lockers no longer crowd the doorways: the stores room has three lockers and Dot's supply locker, and its door to the back rooms moved two tiles over; the Checkpoint guard room's lockers moved to the side wall.
- Level 0 ceiling lights are in order now: rows and columns of lights every 4 tiles across rooms and halls, with about 1 in 7 dead, instead of a random 1 in 20 floor tiles. Only newly generated areas change. (The first version of this placed the grid before the rooms, which painted over it and left Level 0 with no lights at all.)
- The Breakroom is no longer marked on your map when you first reach Level 1 (and the "arrow on the pillar" message is gone). Find it by exploring, or from notes and people. Breakroom Hire still starts you there with it marked.
- Level 2 is harder, as a Class 3 level: about twice as many monsters per tunnel square, about half of them the Backrooms' own entities (more Hounds and Smilers, Wretches from day 20, Skin-stealers from day 10), and canned food in junk heaps half as often.
- Breakroom job rewards fit the job better: Thin the Hounds and Who they were also pay first aid, Supply drop also pays tools, and battery rewards are a few loose cells instead of whole boxes.
- Loot is now a themed handful instead of a whole store. Every crate, locker, junk heap and shelf on Levels 1 and 2 picks **one** kind of thing (tools, hardware, car stuff, lights, batteries, first aid, food or snacks) and holds 3 to 6 of it. Before, some piles pulled in the base game's full toolbox, battery box or vending-machine sets and could hold dozens of items.
- Redrew the Level 1 and Level 2 map sprites in an UndeadPeople style: textured ground for the garage, dead zones and tunnels, and landmarks shown as signboards with a pictogram (a rippling wall for arrival alcoves, a steaming mug for the Breakroom, sandbags and fire for squatter camps, bloodied sandbags for overrun camps, elevator doors for lobbies, a ladder, a boiler, a valve wheel, a water drop and a hard hat for the Tunnel Crew).
- The Level 1 and Level 2 map sprites now also show with the UndeadPeople tileset, not just UltiCa.
- Level 2 has far fewer leaking steam joints: about 1 tunnel square in 4 has one (it was about one per square), so the tunnels are hot but not scalding everywhere.
- UltiCa fallback sprites for almond water and its bottle, and overmap sprites for Level 1 squares (garage), the Breakroom (shelter) and the Level 0 rippling-wall room (an ordinary Level 0 chamber).
- You now land where you are (always at ground level on Level 1) when crossing between Level 0 and Level 1. You land just inside the nearest rippling wall within 30 tiles (including the room you came in through); only if there is none does a new alcove or thin-walled room form around you. Leaving Level 1 brings you out under where you were, not where you went in.

### Fixed
- The Exchange traders and the All-Nite cook had almost nothing to sell (their stock was bought on a tiny budget); they now lay out their whole stock every restock.
- Breakroom runners never set off in a real game: the Breakroom map didn't give Jonah and Rosa the ids the runner schedule looks them up by. New worlds only (runners placed before this fix keep standing still).
- When Jonah or Rosa died, their death was never recorded (an error instead): the runner board now crosses them out and the other runner can still go out.
- Level 2 no longer shows a mapgen error ("setting terrain to t_br2_light ... when item ... existed") when a tunnel light lands on a junk pile.
- Breakroom runners never left on a run: picking where to go always failed, so every run was put off 6 hours, forever. Their travel also always pointed at their own spot, and the "Find the runner" job marked your own position instead of the runner's. Runners now leave at the time on the runner board and walk off even while you watch.
- Level 1 structures no longer count towards the main world's limit on required overmap specials. The elevator lobbies would have pushed the main world over that limit.
- Cars can no longer block an arrival alcove: any car touching the alcove or the 3 tiles in front of its opening is removed (the parking bays elsewhere keep all six car spots).
- Followers now arrive with you through rippling walls (they were left stranded where you first landed). Followers within 20 tiles come along and land on free tiles next to you.
